# Forum principles

These are decision filters. If a screen, component, or campaign brand overlay violates a principle, the principle wins.

## 1. Earn trust on every screen

Political products start at a deficit. Users assume spin.

- Show who is speaking, who paid, and when the data was current.
- Prefer named sources over atmosphere.
- Do not dress unofficial tools as government property.
- Photographs of people require consent context in field products.

Trust is a layout problem: identity, provenance, and next action must be visible without a hunt.

## 2. Make the next civic action obvious

Every public surface has one primary act: donate, sign, call, RSVP, share, look up, read.

- One primary CTA per viewport.
- Secondary actions are text or quiet buttons.
- Forms ask for the minimum to complete the act. Enrich later.
- Recurring gifts are an explicit choice, never a silent default that hides as one-time.

## 3. Tell the truth in the interface

The UI is part of the record.

- Dates, jurisdictions, and vote positions are labeled, not implied.
- Countdown clocks only appear when the deadline is real and sourced.
- Polling and “momentum” numbers require methodology nearby.
- Error copy says what failed and what to do. Never “something went wrong” on money or identity.

## 4. Accessible by default

Civic audiences include low vision, motor impairment, older voters, field volunteers on cheap Androids, and screen readers.

- WCAG 2.2 AA is the floor, not a QA phase.
- 4.5:1 body text. 3:1 large text and UI chrome.
- 44×44px minimum hit targets on mobile field tools.
- Do not encode meaning in color alone (bill status, party, alert severity).
- Motion is optional and short. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 5. Density with dignity

Public pages are editorial. Ops and data tools are dense.

- Public: generous type, one story, one act.
- Data: compact rows, sticky context, filters that survive refresh.
- Never shrink type below 14px UI / 16px public body to fit more politics on a screen.

## 6. Brand is a layer

Forum’s base is civic structure. Org identity sits on top.

```
Brand overlay     logo, --brand-primary, photography, voice adjectives
Forum structure   type scale, space, semantics, patterns, compliance
```

An org may be crimson, gold, or forest. They may not redefine “danger,” “success,” or “official” as marketing colors.

## 7. Continuity across channels

The same system must survive:

- Site
- Donation iframe
- Email
- SMS landing
- Paid creative
- Print leave-behind
- Polling-place / event signage

If a token cannot print or survive 4-bit email, it does not belong in the core set.

## 8. Fast enough for the field

Canvassers and donors are on bad networks.

- Public pages: LCP under 2.5s on mid-tier mobile.
- Donation form interactive in under 3s.
- No hero video autoplay on action pages.
- Maps and voter-file widgets lazy-load after the act is possible.

## Content voice

- Plain language. Grade 8 or below on public pages.
- Active verbs: Donate. Call. Sign. Look up. Show up.
- No false intimacy. No all-caps paragraphs. No more than one exclamation per view.
- Disclaimers are legible, not legally buried.
